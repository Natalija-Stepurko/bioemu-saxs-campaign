Biomolecular emulators · solution scattering · experimental design

BioEmu-1 fits 37% of 399 solution-scattering profiles without reweighting, the best of 7 ensemble models, and its disordered ensembles are too extended

Raw BioEmu-1 ensembles against 399 SASBDB profiles · reweighting to measure what the data can resolve · a measurement campaign built from the map

Natalija Stepurko · October 2026 · natalija-stepurko.com

BioEmu-1 [1] emulates a protein's conformational ensemble from its sequence. The next model will be trained with new measurements, and that campaign should start where the current model is wrong and where a measurement can tell. I scored the raw BioEmu-1 ensemble, with no reweighting, against 439 small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) profiles from SASBDB [4], using the public ensembles and back-calculated curves of PeptoneBench [3]; 403 profiles pass data-quality checks and 399 of those have a BioEmu-1 ensemble. The ensemble fits 37% of them at reduced χ² ≤ 2 (median χ² 3.3); the median is 2.3 for folded and 8.9 for disordered proteins. Reweighting each ensemble towards its profile sorts the misses: 65 need a modest correction, 91 need their populations moved, and 97 cannot be fitted from the conformers the model proposes. On the same profiles, 6 other generators fit worse before reweighting. One expectation came out reversed: for disordered proteins the ensemble radius of gyration is 1.21 times the measured value at the median, so the model's disordered ensembles are too extended, a direction no other model in the comparison shares. The campaign proposal built from this map names the systems, the assay, scripted quality criteria and the work packages for an external provider.

All the code is public. Every number and figure on this page comes from the pipeline in this repository, which downloads the public inputs, scores them and writes the campaign proposal.Natalija-Stepurko/bioemu-saxs-campaign

The question

A biomolecular emulator predicts the set of shapes a protein adopts in solution, and how often, from its sequence alone. BioEmu-1 was trained on predicted structures, molecular-dynamics simulations and measured stabilities [1]. Its successors will be trained with new experimental data. Choosing those experiments is the job this study rehearses: where is the model wrong, where can a measurement tell, and what should be commissioned first. SAXS is the measurement examined here, because it reports the overall size and shape distribution of a protein in solution and because 439 curated profiles with sequences are public [4, 3].

Two things were known. BioEmu conformers have served as a pool for SAXS ensemble selection on a few dozen proteins, where the unweighted pool did not reproduce the data and selection was needed [2]. And PeptoneBench ranks ensemble generators, BioEmu among them, by their fit after reweighting [3]. What neither asked is where the raw model fails, by protein class, and what that implies for the next measurements.

The error map

The raw ensemble fits 37% of profiles. Median reduced χ² is 3.3 (quartiles 1.4–10.9). Expectation E1, that disordered proteins fit worse than folded ones, is met (p = < 0.001).

Error and chain length. Expectation E2, that the error of folded proteins rises with chain length, is not met: the rank correlation is weak (Spearman ρ = +0.09, p = 0.16). What the data show is a step, not a slope: folded proteins of up to 100 residues fit 67% of the time (n = 15), longer ones 45% (n = 231), with no further trend above that.

Size, in the direction not expected. Expectation E4 predicted over-compaction of disordered proteins, the usual failure of models trained on folded structures. The data show the reverse: the ensemble radius of gyration is 1.21 times the measured value at the median for disordered proteins, above 1 in 76% of them, against 0.98 for folded proteins. Of the other models, AlphaFold2 1.01, IDPFold 1.00, PepTron 1.01 sit near 1 on the same entries, and Boltz-2 0.52, ESMFold 0.88, idpSAM 0.40 err the other way: BioEmu-1 is the only one whose disordered ensembles are too extended, which makes them a direct target for new data.

What SAXS can and cannot resolve

A poor raw fit has three possible meanings, and the reweighting path tells them apart. The ensemble's conformer weights are moved towards the data by maximum-entropy reweighting [7], with decreasing strength of the prior, and χ² is tracked against the effective sample fraction φ that survives. An entry that reaches χ² ≤ 2 while keeping half its effective sample needs a calibration; one that reaches it only after φ falls below 0.5 needs its populations moved; one that never reaches it is unresolved by SAXS from the conformers the model proposes.

For the campaign. New SAXS data moves the model most on population cases, and least on calibration cases, which a consistent correction handles. Unresolved cases need a measurement that reports local structure, because SAXS says the ensemble is wrong without saying how. Expectation E3 is not met: 10% of profiles reach χ² ≈ 1 while keeping more than 30% of the effective sample.

Other models on the same profiles

The same archive carries back-calculated curves from other generators for the same entries, scored here identically and without reweighting: AlphaFold2, Boltz-2, ESMFold, IDPFold, idpSAM, PepTron. Five further folders are not used: Boltz-1x and ESMFlow are superseded by models already included, idpGAN and IDP-o are disorder-only generators represented here by idpSAM, and PepTron-base is the un-finetuned PepTron. BioEmu-1 has the lowest median raw χ² of the 7; the single-structure predictors (AlphaFold2, ESMFold) cannot be reweighted, so their best fit is their raw fit.

The measurement campaign

The proposal ranks candidate systems by the model's error, by whether SAXS can resolve it and by tractability (chain length, disorder class), then sets out the assay, the quality criteria that a script applies to every dataset, the success criteria, the contingencies and the work packages for an external provider. Every candidate's measured molecular weight matches its sequence mass within the monomer range, so the model's error is not an oligomer the sequence cannot reveal.

The assay is size-exclusion-coupled SAXS at three concentrations with a protein standard in every session, tags removed, one standard buffer recorded with each dataset, and a repeat of one deposited entry per batch as a cross-site control. Six quality criteria (Guinier linearity, no low-angle upturn, no radiation damage across frames, molecular weight from I(0) within 20% of the sequence mass, no negative intensities, control reproduced at χ² < 2) decide pass or fail from recorded values. Unresolved cases get hydrogen–deuterium exchange mass spectrometry on the same batch of protein. Every batch of twelve constructs re-runs this pipeline on its new profiles, so the error map is updated before the next batch is chosen.

Approach

The 439 profiles, with sequences, pH and per-residue disorder scores, are PeptoneDB-SAXS [3], curated from SASBDB [4]. The BioEmu-1 ensembles and their back-calculated curves (Pepsi-SAXS [6]) are the PeptoneBench predictions; this study re-analyses them and does not re-sample. Each raw fit scales the ensemble average to the data with one factor [5] and one constant background and reports reduced χ² against the experimental errors. The Guinier radius of gyration is fitted on the low-angle region (q·Rg ≤ 1.3), iterated, with the fit taken from the upper part of the window, so a low-angle upturn registers as an aggregation flag and does not inflate Rg. Profiles with an upturn above 10%, an intensity more than three standard errors below zero, or no valid Guinier region are reported but kept out of every statistic (1, 12 and 7 profiles). BioEmu-1 models monomers, so each entry's measured molecular weight from the SASBDB record is compared with its sequence mass; 18 entries measure more than 1.6 times their sequence mass and are treated as oligomers and excluded (their median raw χ² is 9.8). Disorder classes use the mean per-residue score: folded below 0.2, disordered above 0.6.

Limits

The ensembles were generated by the PeptoneBench authors with BioEmu-1 at their settings; a re-sampling with the current checkpoint is the first check once GPU time is available. SASBDB profiles differ in buffer, temperature and construct, none of which the model sees, so part of the raw error is condition mismatch, which a campaign under one standard condition removes. The forward model is treated as exact; its hydration-shell parameters bias Rg by a few per cent, which is why calibration cases are read with care. Disorder classes come from a sequence-based predictor, not from the data. The comparison between models uses only the entries each archive folder provides.
