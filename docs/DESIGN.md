# Design: where BioEmu-1 disagrees with solution scattering, and what to measure next

*Written before any score was computed. Sections 1–6 are the fixed design; later sections, if any, are dated.*

## 1. The question

BioEmu-1 (Lewis et al. 2025) emulates the equilibrium conformational ensemble of a protein from its
sequence. The next model in the series will be trained with new experimental measurements, which
have to be chosen, commissioned and quality-controlled. That choice should start from where the
current model is wrong, and from where a measurement could tell. This study builds that map for one
measurement type, small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS), and turns it into a measurement proposal.

Three questions, each with a fixed output:

1. **Where does the raw BioEmu-1 ensemble disagree with existing SAXS data?** The raw ensemble is the
   model's prediction. Scored without reweighting, against 439 experimental profiles, by protein class.
2. **How much of each disagreement can SAXS resolve?** Reweighting the ensemble to the data shows how
   far the ensemble's populations must move to match, and how much the profile constrains them.
3. **Which proteins should be measured next, with which assay?** A ranked list with the reasoning, and
   a campaign proposal: systems, constructs, assays, controls, QC criteria, success criteria,
   contingencies and outsourced work packages.

## 2. What is already known

- Haitin et al. (2026, *Protein Science*) used BioEmu as a conformer pool for ensemble selection
  against SAXS on five in-house proteins and 20 SASBDB entries. They report that the unweighted pool
  average did not reproduce the experimental curve in their worked example (χ² 71 before selection,
  0.66 after) and that BioEmu does not model pH, temperature, ligands or oligomers. They did not map
  the raw error across protein classes or ask what to measure.
- PeptoneBench (Invernizzi et al. 2025) benchmarks ensemble generators, BioEmu among them, on 439
  SASBDB profiles and 659 chemical-shift sets, reporting root-mean-square error before and after
  maximum-entropy reweighting. Its ensembles and back-calculated curves are public (Zenodo
  17306061, CC-BY 4.0). The benchmark ranks models; it does not analyse the error by protein class
  or derive an experimental plan.
- Simple baselines match foundation models on several protein tasks (Kedzierska et al. 2025 for
  single-cell models; for ensembles, the comparison is open).

What this study adds: a class-resolved error map of the raw BioEmu-1 ensemble against SAXS, an
analysis of how much the measurement constrains each case, and an experimental-campaign proposal
built from the two.

## 4. Measures

For each entry, with the back-calculated curve of each conformer and the experimental curve:

- **Raw fit.** The uniform ensemble average is scaled to the experimental curve (one scale factor,
  one constant background) and scored by reduced χ² against experimental errors. This is the
  prediction error. Also the radius of gyration (Rg) of the ensemble versus the Guinier Rg of the
  data.
- **Reweighted fit.** Maximum-entropy reweighting of the conformer weights to fit the data, with the
  PeptoneBench procedure and regularisation. Reported: χ² after reweighting, and the effective
  sample size fraction φ (the Kish fraction of conformers kept). A low φ means the data force the
  ensemble far from its prior.
- **Resolvability.** How much the profile constrains the ensemble: the drop in χ² per unit of weight
  entropy lost, and whether the reweighted Rg distribution differs from the raw one beyond the
  experimental Rg uncertainty. An entry whose raw χ² is high but needs only a small reweighting is a
  calibration problem; one that needs φ far below 1 is a population problem; one that cannot be fit
  at all is outside the model's reach or has a data problem.
- **Protein class.** Folded (disorder fraction < 0.2), partly disordered (0.2–0.6), disordered
  (> 0.6), by the per-residue disorder scores supplied; chain length bins; multi-domain flag where
  obtainable.
- **Data quality flags.** Guinier range validity, signs of aggregation (upturn at low q) and of
  over-subtraction (an intensity more than three standard errors below zero; points within noise of
  zero at high angle are normal), so that model error is not confused with data error. Entries that
  fail a flag are kept but marked.

## 5. Pre-specified expectations

Written before scoring, to be reported as met or not met:

- E1. Raw χ² will be worse for disordered than for folded proteins, because the model was trained
  mostly on folded structures and molecular dynamics of them.
- E2. Among folded proteins, raw χ² will rise with chain length and domain count.
- E3. For most entries the reweighted fit will reach χ² near 1 with φ above 0.3, i.e. SAXS constrains
  but does not overturn the ensemble; a minority will need φ below 0.1.
- E4. The ensemble Rg will be systematically smaller than the experimental Rg for disordered
  proteins (over-compaction), the known failure of models trained on folded data.

## 6. The campaign proposal

From the error map and the resolvability analysis, the proposal names:

- the classes of protein where the model is most wrong and SAXS can tell, which is where new SAXS
  data would move the model most;
- the classes where SAXS cannot discriminate, which need a different measurement (NMR relaxation,
  HDX-MS, single-molecule FRET), with the reason;
- a ranked candidate list of specific proteins for measurement, with construct notes, expected
  information gain and feasibility;
- the assay design: buffer and concentration series, standards, controls, replicate structure;
- QC criteria that can be applied by script, not by eye; success criteria; contingencies;
- work packages for an external provider, with milestones and what is reviewed at each.

The proposal is the deliverable the error map exists for.

## 7. Post hoc additions and renamings (2026-10-09)

Added after the first full run and its results were seen. The four expectations and §1–§6 stay as
written above; nothing below changes a pre-registered quantity, and every new quantity is written
beside the old ones in `results/`.

- **Renamed kinds.** The four labels along the reweighting path are now *raw fit*, *modestly
  reweightable* (χ² ≤ 2 with φ ≥ 0.5), *strongly reweightable* (χ² ≤ 2 only with φ < 0.5) and *not fit
  along the path* (formerly fits / calibration / population / unresolved). The logic and the counts
  at the pre-specified threshold are unchanged; the counts at φ thresholds 0.3 and 0.7 are reported as a
  sensitivity. The interpretation of the kinds is given as interpretation, with two cautions: φ measures
  how concentrated the weights become, not information content, and SAXS is low-dimensional, so distinct
  ensembles can give the same curve. The `by_class.csv` column `share_unresolved` became `share_not_fit`.
- **Rigour on the size finding (B).** Bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples) of the median Rg ratio per
  class; a two-sided Wilcoxon on the log ratio for disordered entries beside the pre-registered one-sided
  test; a sensitivity dropping the five most extreme ratios; Spearman of the ratio against length within
  the disordered class; a near-duplicate check (5-mer Jaccard ≥ 0.5) with the statistics recomputed on one
  value per cluster; and the radius of gyration computed from the Cα coordinates of the sampled conformers
  (stage `coordrg`) for every clean disordered entry and 40 random folded ones, to tell whether the excess
  size sits in the conformers or in the forward model.
- **Robustness of the headline (C).** Bootstrap intervals for the raw-fit share overall and per class and
  for each paired model comparison; a second fit score that does not weight by the reported errors (the
  normalised root-mean-square deviation of ln I over the usable q range, defined in `saxs.nrmsd_log`),
  applied to the raw and the reweighted curve, with the class and model orderings checked under it. The
  model comparison names the ensemble generators as the primary comparison and the single-structure
  predictors as reference baselines, and restricts its claim to the six comparators on the shared
  entries; AF-CALVADOS is cited as a further method evaluated on this benchmark whose predictions were not
  in the archive.
- **Two validation experiments (D).** `select_eval`: a retrospective evaluation of the selection rule on
  the 399 profiles as a pool, with random, predicted-error and predicted-priority policies against the
  observed-priority ceiling on 5-fold splits grouped by sequence cluster. `predict`: predictors of
  log10 raw χ² and of the kind from pre-acquisition features only, with baselines, linear models and small
  gradient-boosted trees, grouped 5-fold cross-validation repeated five times, permutation importance and
  a calibration check. Both are post hoc and retrospective; their outcome (not predictive; no better than
  chance) is reported as found.
- **Campaign in two arms (E).** Arm 1, model improvement by replication of high-error reweightable
  monomers under one standard condition (no novel-acquisition list, because the error predictor is not
  predictive); arm 2, a pre-specified test of the extension bias on disordered proteins chosen from the
  model's own ensemble Rg against the scaling-law Rg, with matched controls. The headline became the
  question form, the method diagram and the expectations table were moved before the results, and the
  checkpoint is named as the archive records it (BioEmu at code commit ac7455d; checkpoint version not
  recorded; re-sampling with v1.2 is the first follow-up).
- **Worked examples.** One entry per class, the one nearest the class median raw χ², with a DENSS ab
  initio envelope from the measured curve (stage `envelopes`, run in a separate environment because DENSS
  needs numpy < 2) and the highest-weight conformer docked by principal axes.

## 8. Conventions

- Every number on the results page is read from `results/` at build time.
- Every stage writes a parameter record with the inputs' checksums and the code commit.
- No reweighting result is used to choose which raw results to report.
- Prose follows a cited-paper register; the page carries no process bookkeeping.
